The `Immersion/` folder currently contains 94 Markdown files. The lesson generator found 68 usable source lessons, 1,904 vocabulary items, and 1,203 immersion passages. It also imports 28 lesson-note files, two verb JSON files, plus phrase and word lists from `my-basic-lessons/` and `my-translations/`. Together they feed 106 numbered lessons, 43 topic lessons in Library, 2,840 numbered-lesson variation cards, 6,864 story-practice cards, and six dedicated word-drill sets with 5,938 drill items.

Yes: mixing structured lessons with unstructured videos is ideal. Structured practice gives you handles for grammar and vocabulary. Video immersion gives you rhythm, accent, emotion, and messy real context. The trick is not to split them into separate worlds.

## Install For Offline Study

Open `https://shaunvaughn.com/sinhala/` for device-specific installation steps, then launch the app while online. Keep the first session open until the header reports **Offline ready**; that status appears only after the full app, lesson data, and linked study documents are available to its service worker. Reopen the installed app once while still online before relying on airplane-mode study. Progress remains in that browser, and clearing site data can remove both progress and offline files.

## Course Shape

The app does not ask you to pick a story before every session. Its main path is simply Lesson 1, Lesson 2, Lesson 3, and onward. Each lesson mixes several sources and has four short sub-lessons:

1. **Warm-up**: recognize and match Sinhala and English.
2. **Grammar**: learn one compact pattern and identify it in a bilingual sentence.
3. **Conversation**: understand a dialogue turn, then choose or produce a reply.
4. **Immersion**: build a translation word by word, then work with a course-topic phrase or real script line through breakdown, response, or speaking.

Each numbered lesson now has a larger variation bank instead of one fixed short card list. Basic and Foundation rounds target 10 questions, Story and Conversation rounds target 11, and Advanced rounds target 12. Every pool contains at least a full round of cards from the visible practical topic. At runtime, the four parts prefer those topic cards while Immersion deliberately keeps one real-script activity; small grammar, conversation, and due-review extensions preserve cumulative learning without replacing the lesson theme. Topic vocabulary can be recognized in both directions, and word-only topics receive a topic-specific production prompt so they do not fall back to unrelated questions. A numbered lesson must be cleared through three varied rounds before the app advances. The material becomes more advanced across Basic, Foundation, Story, Conversation, and Advanced bands, but nothing is locked. You may skip ahead, go back, or review.

Grammar recognition keeps selection and submission separate: the chosen phrase receives a visible outline before **Submit** checks it. Submission locks every phrase, including after a missed pattern, while **Next** remains the action for moving forward.

The complete curriculum includes 103 grammar structures with a larger rotating bank of authored example families, 30 conversation scenarios with deeper dialogue variation, and 1,010 expansion or imported vocabulary items in reusable packs. All 103 structures now appear in the numbered course. The [Course Library](course-library.md) now supplies 43 topics in total: 23 ordered built-in course topics, 15 imported note topics, and 5 verb-guide lessons generated from the 100-verb JSON set. Each topic has a focused review, while its word and phrase inventory can recur through spaced repetition. The imported “Because, and, or, too” topic includes a curated Sinhala-script block so its cause, coordination, alternative, and additive examples remain attached to that named topic. The verb-guide lessons show morphology rules, curated Sinhala-script verb forms, original romanized alternatives, and three short usage examples for each verb form in a dedicated study section; tap an example to reveal its complete English sentence translation. Each guide also contrasts a regular `-නවා` present/nonpast sentence (“he/she eats”) with a `-මින් ඉන්නවා` ongoing sentence (“he/she is eating”). Want, like, and ability examples use the natural dative subject (`මට`, `එයාට`, or `අපිට`) before the helper. The same transliteration setting used elsewhere controls whether eligible verb-guide and drill text displays in Sinhala script or romanization. Use [Grammar and Conversations](grammar-and-conversations.md) for the full structure index, and consult [Language Sources](language-sources.md) for the research basis and linguistic caveats. Treat all `AI*` examples and words as study drafts and check subtle usage with a native speaker when possible.

Stories still matter as an optional second route. **Stories** lets you open a full translated script, click unfamiliar words, inspect sentence structure, copy lines, or run practice grouped only around that story. Story practice now has three varied rounds like numbered lessons. Each round randomly samples from that story's own vocabulary, matching, sentence-building, fill-blank, structure, response, and speaking cards; due spaced-repetition items and unseen story cards are preferred so repeated reviews eventually move through the full available story bank. Where a story has suitable short bilingual lines or phrase notes, sentence builders appear in both Sinhala-to-English and English-to-Sinhala directions. A reader toggle can also regroup an open script by detected grammar difficulty at the sentence level, using ranked grammar-rule matches so easier sentences appear before harder ones; pressing it again returns to the original script order. After story practice, choose **Continue** for a fresh set from that same story or **Exit** to return to Stories; it will not jump into the numbered course.

**Library** is a third route for direct study. Choose a level, search a topic or word, expand the lesson notes, and launch a concise topic review. Each focused review selects from the topic's broader practice bank, favoring cards you have not seen since opening the app when you continue. If only a few unseen cards remain, that continued review becomes shorter rather than repeat a question. Topic-review completion is tracked separately, while every scored answer updates the same concept-based spaced-repetition schedule used by numbered lessons. Finishing a review leaves that topic selected so you can choose **Continue** for another pass or **Exit** back to Library.

## Daily 25-Minute Routine

1. **Warm-up, 3 minutes**
   Complete the review items the app places first. A missed item returns quickly; remembered items spread out over days and weeks.

2. **Continuous course, 10 minutes**
   Complete one or two numbered lessons. Let the app mix vocabulary, grammar, response, and speaking practice.

3. **Free watch, 5 minutes**
   Watch any current video without pausing. Try to notice words or structures from recent lessons.

4. **Stories and shadowing, 5 minutes**
   Open the script in Stories. Choose 2-4 lines, inspect their structure, then repeat them at natural speed.

5. **Second watch, 2 minutes**
   Watch again for meaning. Do not translate every word.

## Skill Progression

The app cycles through the full grammar reference instead of exhausting a topic in one sitting. Each level revisits earlier patterns in harder conversations and real script lines.

The new pattern-family corpus means one grammar target can reappear across many tightly related sentences. Expect the same frame to return with a different subject, object, time word, or place phrase so you learn the structure instead of memorizing one line.

Inside Practice, lesson-support translations are hidden by default. Reveal them only when needed so you can try to parse the Sinhala first.

The **Words** tab is for direct word-translation reps. It draws from scripts, imported notes, imported verb forms, the built-in course library, and expansion packs. Use it when you want short vocabulary-only reps without leaving the app's main practice flow. When a set ends, choose **Continue** to practice that same set again or **Exit** to return to Words.

Sinhala script is still the default. About 20% of eligible practice prompts can appear in transliteration to help bridging if you need it, and that rate can be lowered to 0% or raised from the Words screen.

### Unit 1: Survival Story Grammar

Goal: understand who did what.

- Pronouns and people: මම, ඔයා, එයා, ඇය, ඔහු
- Possession: මගේ, ඔයාගේ, ඇගේ
- Past events: වුනා, ගියා, කළා
- Common connectors: ඉතින්, ඒත්, ඊට පස්සේ

In the app this corresponds to the **Basic** numbered lessons.

### Unit 2: Wants, Needs, Feelings, and Case Roles

Goal: understand motivation in stories.

- ඕනේ for want/need
- කැමති and ආසයි for liking
- දුක හිතුනා and සතුටුයි for emotional state
- Negative desire: ඕනේ නැහැ
- Dative experiencers: `මට`, `ඔයාට`, and `එයාට` for wanting, needing, liking, ability, feelings, and many bodily states
- Recipient and destination targets: `අම්මට පොත දුන්නා`, `ගෙදරට යනවා`
- Direct-object pronouns: `මාව`, `ඔයාව`, and `එයාව`, distinct from dative forms such as `මට`

The Library's **මම, මට, මාව: everyday case roles** topic compares these forms directly. Grammar-role tags are visible in Library to identify the pattern a phrase is practicing; they support explanation and search, not grading.

In the app this corresponds to the **Foundation** numbered lessons.

### Unit 3: Dialogues

Goal: follow speaker turns.

- Question words: ඇයි, කවුද, මොකක්ද, කොහෙද
- Commands and requests
- Polite versus casual speech
- Repeated character names and pronouns

In the app this corresponds to the **Story** and **Conversation** levels.

### Unit 4: Real Speech

Goal: tolerate speed and imperfect audio.

- Fillers and discourse markers: එතකොට, මෙහෙමයි, මචන්
- Spoken contractions
- Place names and proper nouns
- Meaning from context instead of word-by-word translation

In the app this corresponds to the **Advanced** level.

## Sentence Breakdown Checklist

For any line, ask:

1. Is there a connector? Look for ඉතින්, ඒත්, ඊට පස්සේ, හැබැයි, එතකොට.
2. Who or what is the subject?
3. Is there a time or place phrase?
4. Is there a noun marked with -ට, -ගේ, -ව, or -න්?
5. What is the final verb?
6. Is the meaning negated by නැහැ, නෑ, බෑ, or එපා?

Sinhala often feels easier once you stop expecting English word order. Wait for the verb.

## Translation Workflow

The app imports full English translations already written under Sinhala lines. Missing passages are machine translated and marked `AI*`. The marker means the English is useful study support but has not been personally verified, especially when the Sinhala transcript itself came from imperfect speech recognition.

Review inside the app:

1. Open **Translations**.
2. Choose **Words**, **Phrases**, or **Words and phrases**, then keep the **AI* needs review** filter selected.
3. Compare the Sinhala, English, and matching video moment.
4. Correct the English if needed.
5. Tap **Approve and remove AI*** when the meaning is trustworthy.
6. Export edits occasionally so your work can move to another device.

You can also uncheck **Keep the AI* marker** and tap **Save**. Resetting an edit restores the generated translation and marker.

When importing new scripts, run `npm run build:data`, `npm run translate:data`, and `npm run build:data` again. The middle command sends only pending Sinhala passages to Google Translate.

## How to Add New Videos

1. Save the transcript as a Markdown file in `Immersion/`.
2. Add vocabulary with this format:

   ```md
   - Sinhala word = English meaning
   ```

3. Keep speaker labels on their own line:

   ```md
   - Mother
   ```

4. Keep timestamps on their own line when possible:

   ```md
   1:45
   ```

5. Run `npm run build:data`, `npm run translate:data`, and `npm run build:data` to refresh the app and translate new passages.

## What To Avoid

- Do not turn every line into a flashcard.
- Do not watch only with subtitles.
- Do not spend a whole session on dictionary lookup.
- Do not wait until you understand everything before rewatching.

The win condition is repeated contact with meaningful Sinhala.
