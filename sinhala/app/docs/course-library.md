# Sinhala Course Library

This reference is generated from the dedicated topic lessons used by the app. Each lesson is intentionally short: learn a word set, notice linked grammar, read useful phrases, and perform the two-turn dialogue. In the app, each focused topic review also asks you to build one English-to-Sinhala sentence and one Sinhala-to-English sentence word by word. These topics cycle through the numbered course, where their words and phrases are mixed with script-derived practice and later return through spaced repetition. AI* marks AI-authored Sinhala or English that should be checked by a fluent speaker and can be edited in the app.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive to Present / Nonpast | root + anna - root + anava | Standard conversion of the infinitive form ending in -anna/inna/enna to the present or habitual nonpast -anava/inava/enava. | Intransitive/Passive marker -enava | root + enava - root + una | Verbs ending in -enava typically indicate an involuntary action, passive state, or motion, and often take a past tense ending in -una. | vetenava - vetuna |

**Simple present versus ongoing action:** The -නවා form is the regular or habitual present. Use -මින් ඉන්නවා when you want to emphasize that the action is happening right now.

**Pattern:** habitual: verb + නවා · ongoing: verb + මින් ඉන්නවා

- එයා හැමදාම බත් කනවා. (Eya hemadama bath kanava.) = He/she eats rice every day.
- එයා දැන් බත් කමින් ඉන්නවා. (Eya den bath kamin innava.) = He/she is eating rice now.

**Simple present versus ongoing action:** The -නවා form is the regular or habitual present. Use -මින් ඉන්නවා when you want to emphasize that the action is happening right now.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive to Present / Nonpast | root + anna - root + anava | Standard conversion of the infinitive form ending in -anna/inna/enna to the present or habitual nonpast -anava/inava/enava. This is not a dedicated English-style continuous form; use a -මින් ඉන්නවා construction to make an ongoing action explicit. | kanna - kanava |

**Simple present versus ongoing action:** The -නවා form is the regular or habitual present. Use -මින් ඉන්නවා when you want to emphasize that the action is happening right now.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive to Present / Nonpast | root + anna - root + anava | Standard conversion of the infinitive form ending in -anna/inna/enna to the present or habitual nonpast -anava/inava/enava. This is not a dedicated English-style continuous form; use a -මින් ඉන්නවා construction to make an ongoing action explicit. | kanna - kanava |

**Simple present versus ongoing action:** The -නවා form is the regular or habitual present. Use -මින් ඉන්නවා when you want to emphasize that the action is happening right now.

**Pattern:** habitual: verb + නවා · ongoing: verb + මින් ඉන්නවා

- එයා හැමදාම බත් කනවා. (Eya hemadama bath kanava.) = He/she eats rice every day.
- එයා දැන් බත් කමින් ඉන්නවා. (Eya den bath kamin innava.) = He/she is eating rice now.

#### Verb Study Guide

Source: my-basic-lessons/SinhalaVerbRules_Expanded.json + my-basic-lessons/SinhalaVerbsData_100.json

| Rule | Pattern | Meaning | Example or note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive to Present / Nonpast | root + anna - root + anava | Standard conversion of the infinitive form ending in -anna/inna/enna to the present or habitual nonpast -anava/inava/enava. This is not a dedicated English-style continuous form; use a -මින් ඉන්නවා construction to make an ongoing action explicit. | kanna - kanava |
| Conjunctive Participle Formation | root + la / illa | Formation of the 'having done' or 'after doing' form, often ending in -la or -illa. | Irregular roots like ya- (to go) change significantly to gihilla. |
| Past Tense Regular Transformation | rootmod + uva/a/una | Most past tense forms involve changing the root vowel (e.g., a - e) and appending a suffix like -uva, -a, or -una. | kapanna - kapuva |
| Intransitive/Passive marker -enava | root + enava - root + una | Verbs ending in -enava typically indicate an involuntary action, passive state, or motion, and often take a past tense ending in -una. | vetenava - vetuna |

**Simple present versus ongoing action:** The -නවා form is the regular or habitual present. Use -මින් ඉන්නවා when you want to emphasize that the action is happening right now.

**Pattern:** habitual: verb + නවා · ongoing: verb + මින් ඉන්නවා

## Research Basis

The lesson scope was checked against the following course and grammar sources. Examples are newly authored for this project rather than copied from the sources.

- [Sinhala: Basic Course, Module 1: Beginning Signs and Letters](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED192569.pdf) - U.S. Foreign Service Institute / ERIC; used for script recognition, sound contrasts, and staged beginner reading.
- [Sinhala: Basic Course, Module 2: General Conversation](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED192570.pdf) - U.S. Foreign Service Institute / ERIC; used for short thematic cycles, classroom language, money, buses, daily routine, and travel.
- [Sinhala: Basic Course, Module 3: Sinhala Structures](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED192571.pdf) - U.S. Foreign Service Institute / ERIC; used for noun classes, cases, definiteness, verb forms, particles, numerals, style, and dialect.
- [Certificate Course in Basic Sinhala Level I](https://www.codl.jfn.ac.lk/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Certificate-course-in-Basic-Sinhala-Level-01.pdf) - University of Jaffna, Centre for Open and Distance Learning; used for alphabet, pronouns by case, present/past/future verbs, greetings, and kinship sequencing.
- [Introduction to Spoken Sinhala curriculum](https://www.len.jfn.ac.lk/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/13.Linguistics.pdf) - University of Jaffna, Department of Linguistics; used for numerals, colloquial pronouns, negatives, address, measurements, directions, dialogues, and register.
- [A Functional and Categorical Analysis of Particles in Spoken Sinhala](https://doi.org/10.4038/jdrra.v3i2.82) - Journal of Desk Research Review and Analysis; used for case, highlighting, conjunctive, discourse, interrogative, quotative, and negative particle coverage.
